Migrating your website from WordPress to Webflow can be an excellent opportunity to improve your website's design, simplify content management and create a more efficient marketing workflow.

But there's one concern that stops many business owners from making the switch: Will migrating from WordPress to Webflow hurt my SEO?

If your existing website generates organic traffic, leads or sales, the last thing you want is to lose years of search visibility because of broken URLs, missing content or incorrectly configured redirects.

The good news is that a WordPress-to-Webflow migration doesn't automatically mean losing your Google rankings.

With careful planning, proper content migration, accurate 301 redirects and thorough testing, you can reduce the risks associated with moving your website.

In this guide, you'll learn how to migrate WordPress to Webflow step by step, including how to export your existing content, configure Webflow CMS, preserve important SEO elements, migrate images and forms, and monitor your website after launch.

Whether you're a business owner considering a platform change or a developer managing a client migration, this guide will help you approach the process with confidence.

1. Why Migrate From WordPress to Webflow?

WordPress is a powerful content management system, but it isn't necessarily the best fit for every business.

As websites grow, some businesses find themselves managing numerous plugins, dealing with theme compatibility issues or relying on developers to make relatively simple design changes.

Webflow offers an alternative approach by combining visual development, CMS functionality, managed hosting and publishing in one platform.

Benefits of moving to Webflow

Greater visual design control

Webflow allows developers to create custom layouts, responsive components and animations using a visual development environment.

Unlike many traditional WordPress themes, Webflow provides detailed control over page structure and styling without requiring a large collection of design plugins.

Structured content management

Webflow CMS lets you create collections for blog posts, case studies, team members, services and other repeatable content.

Once the collection templates are configured, your marketing team can publish new content without rebuilding individual pages.

Managed hosting

Webflow handles much of the hosting infrastructure, reducing the need to manage traditional server configurations, WordPress core updates and plugin compatibility.

However, your website will still require content maintenance, integration monitoring and periodic technical reviews.

Simplified marketing workflows

Webflow supports forms, CMS-driven landing pages, reusable components and integrations with marketing tools.

For businesses that frequently update their websites, these capabilities can make content and design management more efficient.

When you shouldn't migrate

Moving to Webflow isn't always the right decision.

If your website relies on WooCommerce, complex membership functionality, extensive custom plugins or specialized backend applications, a migration may require substantial redevelopment.

You should also evaluate Webflow's CMS limits, pricing and integration capabilities before committing. See also: Webflow vs. Framer vs. WordPress comparison.

A migration should solve an existing business problem rather than simply follow a design trend.

3. Step 1: Audit Your Existing WordPress Website

A thorough website audit is the foundation of a successful migration.

Before rebuilding anything, document your existing pages, content, technical configuration and important business functionality.

Create an inventory of your existing URLs

List every important page on your WordPress website.

For example:

Existing URL Page type Planned action
/ Homepage Rebuild
/about/ Static page Rebuild
/services/ Service page Rebuild
/blog/ Blog index Rebuild
/blog/example-post/ Blog article Import into CMS
/contact/ Contact page Rebuild
/old-service/ Outdated page Redirect or retire

For small websites, you can create this inventory manually.

For larger websites, use a crawler such as Screaming Frog to identify existing URLs, metadata, headings, internal links, images and canonical URLs.

Identify your highest-performing pages

Use Google Search Console and your analytics platform to identify pages that generate organic traffic, rank for important keywords, attract backlinks or produce qualified leads.

Prioritize these pages during migration.

For example, if an existing service page generates most of your website inquiries, preserve its useful content and search intent rather than replacing it with a visually attractive but less informative page.

Audit your existing SEO

Record the following information for important pages:

  • SEO titles and meta descriptions.
  • H1 headings and content structure.
  • Canonical URLs.
  • Indexing directives.
  • Structured data.
  • Image alt text.
  • Internal and external links.
  • Organic traffic and search queries.

Export your current sitemap and preserve a copy of your existing robots.txt configuration.

Audit WordPress functionality

Document the functionality provided by your theme and plugins.

Common examples include contact forms, booking systems, ecommerce, memberships, search, multilingual content, CRM integrations and analytics.

For each feature, determine whether it can be recreated using Webflow's native capabilities, an integration or custom development.

4. Step 2: Back Up Your WordPress Website

Never begin a migration without creating a reliable backup.

Your backup should include the WordPress database, uploaded media, themes, plugins and relevant configuration files.

You can create a backup through your hosting provider or a trusted WordPress backup tool.

Store the backup somewhere independent of your WordPress installation.

Export your WordPress content

In your WordPress dashboard, navigate to:

Tools → Export → All Content → Download Export File

WordPress generates an XML file containing supported content such as posts, pages, categories, tags and custom fields.

However, this export is not a complete website backup.

It doesn't independently preserve every aspect of your hosting environment, theme functionality or plugin configuration.

Export additional information

Depending on your website, you may need separate exports for SEO metadata, custom fields, form submissions, ecommerce records and plugin-specific data.

Also preserve your media library and any downloadable resources.

Keep your existing WordPress website operational until the new Webflow website has been published and verified.

5. Step 3: Export WordPress Content to CSV

Webflow supports CSV imports for CMS collections.

WordPress, however, exports content in XML format by default.

You'll therefore need to convert your WordPress content into a suitable CSV structure before importing it.

Option A: Use a WordPress export plugin

A suitable WordPress export plugin can allow you to select specific fields and export posts directly to CSV.

For a typical blog migration, export the title, slug, article content, excerpt, publication date, category, author, featured image and relevant SEO metadata.

Check whether your export plugin supports custom fields if your website relies on them.

Option B: Use the WordPress REST API

Developers can retrieve WordPress content programmatically using the REST API.

For example:

https://example.com/wp-json/wp/v2/posts?per_page=100&page=1

This endpoint can retrieve up to 100 posts per request in a typical WordPress installation.

If your website contains more posts, paginate through the remaining results.

Some custom fields and plugin-generated metadata may require additional configuration or separate exports.

Option C: Convert the WordPress XML file

You can also convert your WordPress XML export into CSV.

This approach can be useful when you already have a complete export and want to transform the data without depending on additional WordPress plugins.

Check the resulting file carefully because rich text, shortcodes and plugin-specific content may need additional processing.

Example CSV structure

A simple blog export might contain the following fields:

Title Slug Category Featured Image
Choosing a Website Platform choosing-a-platform Web Design image-01.jpg
Improving Website Speed improve-website-speed Performance image-02.jpg
Website SEO Checklist website-seo-checklist SEO image-03.jpg

Your actual CSV should also contain the complete article content, publication date, excerpt and any additional fields required by your Webflow CMS.

Before importing, check for duplicate slugs, missing values, broken characters, incorrect dates and unsupported formatting.

6. Step 4: Plan Your Webflow CMS Architecture

One of the biggest mistakes during migration is importing content before designing the new CMS structure.

Webflow organizes structured content into collections.

For example, a business website might use separate collections for blog posts, categories, authors, case studies and team members.

Example: Blog collection

WordPress field Suggested Webflow field
Post title Name
Post slug Slug
Article content Rich Text
Excerpt Plain Text
Featured image Image
Publication date Date/Time
Category Reference
Author Reference
SEO title Plain Text
Meta description Plain Text

The exact structure depends on your website and the Webflow CMS features available on your plan.

Create related collections

If your blog uses categories and authors, consider creating separate collections for them.

For example, an Authors collection could include each author's name, biography, profile image and social links.

A Categories collection could contain category names, descriptions and featured images.

You can then connect these collections to your Blog Posts collection using reference fields.

Handle custom post types

If your WordPress website uses custom post types, evaluate each one separately.

A portfolio website might require a Projects collection containing a project title, client, industry, featured image, gallery, description and testimonial.

Some WordPress custom fields may not map directly to Webflow.

In these cases, restructure the content rather than trying to reproduce every detail of the original database.

Check CMS limits

Before importing, confirm that your chosen Webflow plan supports your required number of CMS items, collections and fields.

Large websites may require additional planning or a different subscription tier.

7. Step 5: Rebuild Your Website in Webflow

Once your CMS architecture is ready, begin developing your new website.

You can use a Webflow template or create a custom design.

For businesses with established branding, the goal may be to preserve recognizable visual elements while improving layout, responsiveness and usability.

Establish a design system

Define your typography, colors, spacing, buttons, forms and reusable components.

A consistent design system makes development more efficient and simplifies future maintenance.

Build reusable components

Create reusable navigation, footer, cards, calls to action and other shared elements.

This allows you to update common sections without editing every page individually.

Develop responsive layouts

Test your designs across desktop, tablet and mobile breakpoints.

Pay particular attention to navigation, text wrapping, image proportions, content spacing and interactive elements.

Don't rely solely on Webflow's desktop preview.

Create CMS collection templates

Build templates for your blog posts, case studies and other dynamic content.

Connect each template to the appropriate CMS fields.

For example, your blog template should display the article title, featured image, publication date, author, main content and related articles.

Preserve valuable content

A redesign is an opportunity to improve your website, but avoid unnecessarily removing content that already performs well.

Maintain important topics, useful information and search intent while improving the user experience.

8. Step 6: Import WordPress Content Into Webflow CMS

After creating your collections and templates, you can import your content.

Webflow supports bulk CMS imports through CSV files.

Prepare your CSV

Ensure that each column corresponds to the intended Webflow field.

Remove unnecessary WordPress shortcodes and plugin-specific markup.

Check that your rich text is compatible with your import workflow.

Import your content

In Webflow:

  1. Open the Collections panel.
  2. Create or select your CMS collection.
  3. Choose the CSV import option.
  4. Upload your prepared CSV.
  5. Map the CSV columns to your collection fields.
  6. Review the field mappings.
  7. Complete the import.

Webflow also supports mapping imported content to reference fields, although relationships and imported values should be verified carefully.

Test before importing everything

Start with a small sample.

Import a simple article, an article containing multiple images and a more complex article with lists, links or embedded content.

Review how each appears in your CMS template.

Once the sample looks correct, proceed with the remaining content.

Common CMS import problems

Broken rich text: Remove unsupported WordPress shortcodes and review the source formatting.

Missing images: Confirm that image URLs are accessible and compatible with your import workflow.

Incorrect dates: Standardize date formats before importing.

Broken references: Create dependent collection items before importing content that references them.

Duplicate slugs: Ensure every CMS item has a unique slug.

Missing SEO metadata: Export metadata separately if your WordPress export doesn't include the required fields.

9. Step 7: Migrate Images and Media

Images are often overlooked during website migrations.

Your WordPress website may contain featured images, inline article images, galleries, downloadable PDFs and other media.

Create an inventory of important assets before transferring them.

Featured images

Transfer the original images where possible.

Use suitable image dimensions and formats for the new website.

Avoid unnecessarily enlarging or repeatedly compressing images.

Inline article images

Check that images embedded in your articles still load correctly.

If your imported content references images hosted on your old WordPress server, consider replacing those URLs with assets hosted through your new website.

Otherwise, the images may break when your old hosting is cancelled.

Image galleries

Complex WordPress galleries may require additional processing.

Depending on your Webflow CMS structure, you may need multi-image fields, separate collections or custom components.

Test the gallery implementation before importing large amounts of content.

PDFs and downloadable resources

Re-upload important downloadable files and update their links.

If existing document URLs attract traffic or backlinks, include them in your migration plan.

10. Step 8: Preserve Your SEO With Proper URL Mapping

URL mapping is one of the most important parts of a WordPress-to-Webflow migration.

If your existing website has established search visibility, changing URLs unnecessarily introduces avoidable risk.

Whenever possible, preserve the existing URL structure.

Create a redirect mapping spreadsheet

Document every important old URL and its intended destination.

For example:

Old WordPress URL New Webflow URL Action
/about-us/ /about/ 301 redirect
/our-services/ /services/ 301 redirect
/blog/website-tips/ /blog/website-tips/ Preserve URL
/old-service/ /services/ Redirect if relevant

If an old page has been replaced, redirect it to the most relevant equivalent.

Avoid redirecting unrelated pages to your homepage.

Understand trailing slashes

WordPress and Webflow may handle trailing slashes differently.

For example, your WordPress URL might be:

https://example.com/blog/website-tips/

Your new Webflow URL might be:

https://example.com/blog/website-tips

Test how the new website handles both versions and whether the appropriate canonical URL is generated.

Configure Webflow 301 redirects

In Webflow, navigate to:

Site settings → Publishing → 301 redirects

Add the old path and the new destination.

For example:

Old path: /about-us

Redirect to: /about

Publish your website to activate the redirect.

Webflow also supports importing redirects through CSV files, which is useful for larger migrations.

Important: Importing a new redirect CSV can replace existing redirect rules. Export and preserve your existing rules before uploading an updated file.

Avoid redirect chains

A redirect chain occurs when one URL redirects to another URL that redirects again.

For example:

/old-page → /previous-page → /new-page

Where possible, redirect the original URL directly to the final destination.

This improves efficiency and reduces unnecessary requests.

Keep redirects in place

Google recommends maintaining permanent redirects for at least one year.

Keeping important redirects longer is often beneficial, especially when external websites continue linking to old URLs.

11. Step 9: Transfer SEO Metadata and Structured Data

Your new website should preserve the important SEO information associated with your existing pages.

SEO titles and meta descriptions

Export the SEO titles and meta descriptions from your WordPress website.

In Webflow, configure metadata for static pages and use dynamic CMS fields for collection templates.

For example, your Blog Posts collection can include separate SEO Title and Meta Description fields.

Canonical URLs

Review canonical tags on important pages.

Make sure they point to the intended production URLs rather than staging domains or outdated WordPress URLs.

Structured data

If your WordPress website uses structured data, identify which schema types are relevant.

For example, a business website might use Organization or ProfessionalService markup, while blog posts may use BlogPosting markup.

Recreate the appropriate structured data in Webflow using supported custom code or other suitable implementation methods.

Only include structured data that accurately represents visible content.

XML sitemap

Webflow can automatically generate an XML sitemap for your published website.

Review it after launch and confirm that important pages are included.

Submit your sitemap through Google Search Console.

Robots.txt and indexing

Check that your production website isn't accidentally blocked from indexing.

Review robots.txt and any page-level noindex settings before launch.

12. Step 10: Reconnect Forms, CRM and Marketing Integrations

Your website needs to do more than display content.

Before launching, make sure every important business workflow works correctly.

Contact forms

Rebuild your contact forms in Webflow.

Connect them to the appropriate destination, such as email, HubSpot, Google Sheets or an automation platform.

Submit several test entries and verify that the information reaches the intended destination.

CRM integrations

If your WordPress website previously connected to HubSpot or another CRM, rebuild and test those integrations.

Verify field mappings, consent requirements, notifications and automation triggers.

Booking systems

Reconnect Calendly or your preferred scheduling platform.

Test the complete booking process on desktop and mobile.

Analytics and conversion tracking

Configure Google Analytics, Google Tag Manager or your preferred analytics platform.

Check that page views and important conversion events are recorded correctly.

Avoid introducing duplicate tracking during the migration.

13. Step 11: Test Your Webflow Website Before Launch

Thorough testing reduces the likelihood of discovering serious problems after your website goes live.

Functional testing

Test navigation, internal links, forms, buttons, downloads, CMS pages and interactive components.

Verify that every important user journey works correctly.

Responsive testing

Review your website across desktop, tablet and mobile screen sizes.

Pay particular attention to navigation, typography, spacing, forms and images.

Content testing

Compare migrated content against your original WordPress website.

Check titles, publication dates, featured images, headings, formatting, internal links and downloadable resources.

SEO testing

Verify metadata, canonical URLs, redirects, sitemap configuration and indexing settings.

Use a crawler to identify broken links, missing metadata and unexpected redirects.

Performance testing

Run important pages through Google PageSpeed Insights.

Review image sizes, unnecessary scripts, layout stability and mobile performance.

Focus on meaningful improvements rather than achieving a perfect Lighthouse score.

14. Step 12: Connect Your Domain and Launch

Once your new Webflow website is ready, connect your custom domain.

In Webflow, open your site's publishing settings and follow the instructions for adding a custom domain.

Webflow will provide the DNS records required for your configuration.

Update those records through your domain registrar or DNS provider.

Protect your business email

If your domain also handles business email, avoid changing unrelated MX, SPF, DKIM or DMARC records.

A website migration shouldn't interrupt your email service.

Launch checklist

Before announcing your new website, confirm that:

  • Your domain loads correctly over HTTPS.
  • Important pages are accessible.
  • Old URLs redirect to the correct destinations.
  • Contact forms and integrations work.
  • Analytics and conversion tracking are active.
  • Canonical URLs point to the correct domain.
  • Your sitemap is accessible.
  • Important pages are indexable.
  • Your website works properly on mobile.

Keep your original WordPress website and backups available during the initial post-launch period.

15. Step 13: Monitor Your SEO After Migration

Publishing your new website isn't the end of the migration.

Monitor your website closely during the following weeks and months.

First 48 hours

Check your domain, redirects, forms, analytics and important pages.

Resolve critical errors immediately.

First week

Review Google Search Console for indexing problems, crawl errors and unexpected changes in search visibility.

Inspect your most important landing pages and verify that internal links point to the new URLs.

First month

Compare organic traffic, conversions, indexed pages and Core Web Vitals against your pre-migration baseline.

Investigate significant changes rather than assuming every fluctuation is caused by the migration.

Following months

Continue monitoring your search performance and update redirects, content or technical settings where necessary.

Google notes that search rankings may fluctuate temporarily during a significant site move.

For medium-sized websites, processing the move can take several weeks or longer.

16. Common WordPress to Webflow Migration Mistakes

Several avoidable mistakes can turn a straightforward migration into a costly project.

Migrating without a complete backup

Always preserve a recoverable copy of your original website.

Changing URLs unnecessarily

Preserve existing URLs where practical and use permanent redirects when changes are unavoidable.

Importing content without testing

Import a representative sample before transferring your entire CMS.

Forgetting plugin-dependent functionality

A WordPress export won't automatically reproduce functionality created by plugins.

Audit those features and plan appropriate replacements.

Leaving images on the old hosting server

Ensure that important media remains accessible after your WordPress hosting is discontinued.

Launching without testing forms

A visually attractive website can still lose leads if its forms and integrations don't work.

Cancelling WordPress hosting too early

Keep your original website available until you've confirmed that the migration is complete and all essential data has been preserved.

17. How Much Does a WordPress to Webflow Migration Cost?

Migration costs depend on the website's size, design, content structure and functionality. For wider budgeting context, read our website redesign cost guide.

For planning purposes, a straightforward small business migration might cost approximately $2,000–$5,000.

A larger custom migration involving extensive CMS content, integrations and technical SEO may require $5,000–$15,000 or more.

These are illustrative planning estimates rather than fixed market rates or published Made by Olayinka prices.

The main cost factors include:

  • Number of unique pages.
  • Volume and complexity of CMS content.
  • Custom design requirements.
  • Forms and third-party integrations.
  • SEO migration complexity.
  • Content restructuring.
  • Testing and post-launch support.

A simple five-page website will generally cost less to migrate than a content-heavy website with hundreds of posts and extensive custom functionality.

18. Frequently Asked Questions

Can I migrate WordPress to Webflow automatically?

Some content can be transferred using exports, CSV transformations and import tools.

However, a complete migration generally requires manual work to rebuild layouts, configure CMS collections, reconnect functionality and validate the finished website.

Can I import WordPress blog posts into Webflow?

Yes. You can export WordPress content, convert it into a suitable CSV format and import it into Webflow CMS.

Check rich text, images, dates, categories and references after importing.

Will I lose my Google rankings if I move to Webflow?

Not necessarily.

Preserving valuable content, maintaining existing URLs where possible and implementing correct redirects can reduce migration-related SEO risks.

However, no migration process can guarantee unchanged rankings.

Can I keep my existing domain?

Yes. You can connect your existing domain to Webflow by updating the appropriate DNS records.

You don't need to purchase a new domain simply because you're changing website platforms.

Is Webflow better than WordPress for SEO?

Both platforms can support strong technical SEO.

Webflow provides managed hosting and built-in SEO controls, while WordPress offers extensive customization through plugins and custom development.

Your content quality and technical implementation matter more than the platform alone.

How long does a WordPress to Webflow migration take?

A small website may take two to four weeks, while a larger website with custom design, extensive CMS content and complex integrations can take several weeks or longer.

The timeline depends on project scope, content readiness and testing requirements.

Should I redesign my website during migration?

You can, but combining a complete redesign with a platform migration introduces additional variables.

If preserving search visibility is especially important, consider retaining your most valuable content and URL structure while making design improvements.

Google recommends changing one major element at a time where practical.

Final Thoughts: Is Moving From WordPress to Webflow Worth It?

Migrating from WordPress to Webflow can be a valuable investment when your business needs a modern, custom-designed website and a more streamlined content management experience.

However, a successful migration requires much more than rebuilding your homepage.

You need to audit your existing website, preserve valuable content, plan your CMS architecture, implement redirects, reconnect integrations and thoroughly test the finished website.

With careful planning, you can take advantage of Webflow's visual development capabilities while reducing unnecessary disruption to your existing search visibility and business operations.
