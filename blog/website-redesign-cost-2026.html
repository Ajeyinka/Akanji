Your website might be the first interaction a potential customer has with your business. But what happens when it looks outdated, loads slowly, doesn't work properly on mobile or fails to generate inquiries?

At some point, improving an underperforming website becomes a business priority. That's when most business owners ask the same question:

How much does a website redesign actually cost?

In 2026, a website redesign can cost anywhere from a few hundred dollars for a simple DIY refresh to $50,000 or more for a complex, professionally managed project.

For many small businesses, a practical planning budget is approximately $2,000–$15,000, depending on the website's size, complexity and the professionals involved.

But the number of pages isn't the only thing that determines your budget. Content strategy, custom design, SEO preservation, CMS migration, integrations and ongoing maintenance can significantly affect the final price.

In this guide, we'll break down website redesign costs, explain what you're actually paying for, compare Webflow, Framer and WordPress, and show you how to plan a redesign without unnecessary expenses.

1. How Much Does a Website Redesign Cost in 2026?

The cost depends on whether you're making small visual improvements, rebuilding an existing website or moving to an entirely different platform.

Here are broad planning ranges for common projects.

Redesign type Illustrative budget Best suited for
DIY website refresh $200–$1,000 Simple visual changes
Freelancer-led refresh $500–$3,000 Small websites with an existing structure
Small business redesign $2,000–$7,500 Service businesses and entrepreneurs
Custom business website $5,000–$15,000 Growing businesses requiring custom design

These ranges overlap because a redesign's scope matters more than its label. For example, a five-page website with custom animations, CRM integrations and complex forms can cost more than a fifteen-page website built using reusable templates.

Recent industry pricing guides also show substantial variation between providers. A 2026 guide from Surfaced estimates many small-business redesigns at $2,000–$15,000, while more comprehensive agency projects can exceed $50,000.

The most useful approach is to identify exactly what your website needs before requesting quotes.

2. Website Refresh vs. Redesign vs. Complete Rebuild

Before setting a budget, understand the difference between three commonly confused services.

Website refresh

A website refresh improves the appearance and usability of your existing website without fundamentally changing its structure.

Typical improvements include:

  • Updating typography, colors and imagery.
  • Improving spacing and visual consistency.
  • Refining mobile layouts.
  • Updating buttons and calls to action.
  • Improving existing sections.
  • Making minor performance improvements.

A refresh is often appropriate when your website already performs reasonably well but looks outdated.

Illustrative budget: $500–$3,000.

Complete website redesign

A full redesign involves rethinking how your website looks, works and communicates your business's value.

It may include new page layouts, navigation, content structure, conversion strategy and responsive development.

For example, a consulting company might replace an outdated five-page website with a modern eight-page website featuring dedicated service pages, testimonials, case studies and an integrated booking system.

Illustrative budget: $2,000–$15,000.

Website rebuild or migration

A rebuild involves reconstructing the website, potentially on a different platform.

For example, moving from WordPress to Webflow or Framer may require rebuilding page layouts, recreating CMS collections, transferring content and implementing redirects.

This process can be more expensive than a simple redesign because it involves both development and migration.

Illustrative budget: $3,000–$20,000+, depending on complexity.

The distinction matters because a visual refresh won't necessarily solve structural problems, and a complete rebuild may be unnecessary if your existing website is technically sound.

3. Seven Factors That Determine Website Redesign Costs

Two websites with the same number of pages can have dramatically different development costs.

Here are the main factors that influence pricing.

1. Number of pages and unique layouts

The number of pages matters, but the number of unique layouts is often more important.

Imagine two businesses that each need ten pages.

Business A needs a homepage, four service pages sharing one template, three case studies and two informational pages.

Business B needs ten individually designed pages with unique interactions and content structures.

Business B will generally require more design and development work, even though both websites contain ten pages.

Reusable components and CMS templates can reduce development time while improving consistency.

2. Custom design vs. templates

Templates can reduce costs because much of the visual structure is already available.

However, they may require compromises when your business has specific branding, layout or functionality requirements.

Custom design gives you greater control over the user experience, visual identity and conversion strategy.

It generally requires additional discovery, wireframing, design and development work.

For a new business with a limited budget, a professionally customized template may be sufficient. For an established brand with complex requirements, custom design may be a more appropriate investment.

3. Content creation and copywriting

A visually attractive website won't accomplish much if visitors can't understand what your business offers.

Your redesign may require:

  • Rewriting your homepage.
  • Creating dedicated service pages.
  • Developing case studies.
  • Improving product descriptions.
  • Writing SEO-focused content.
  • Creating clearer calls to action.

Professional copywriting, content strategy, photography and video production may be priced separately.

Before starting, establish whether you or your developer will provide the content.

4. CMS and content migration

If your existing website contains blog posts, case studies, products or other structured content, that information may need to be transferred.

A migration can involve exporting content, cleaning up data, mapping fields, importing images and rebuilding content templates.

Moving fourteen blog posts is generally less complicated than migrating thousands of articles with multiple authors, categories, custom fields and media galleries.

The more complex your content structure, the more time should be allocated to migration and testing.

5. Integrations and custom functionality

A basic contact form is relatively straightforward.

A multi-step form connected to a CRM, email automation platform and booking system requires more planning and testing.

Other features that can increase costs include ecommerce, payment gateways, membership systems, advanced filtering, custom calculators and API integrations.

Create a list of essential features before requesting a quote. Separate must-have functionality from features that can be introduced after launch.

6. SEO and website performance

An existing website may already attract visitors through Google.

A redesign should preserve valuable URLs, metadata and content wherever appropriate. When URLs change, redirects need to be planned and tested.

Technical SEO work may include a site audit, redirect mapping, metadata migration, sitemap configuration, structured data and post-launch monitoring.

Performance optimization may also require image compression, font optimization, script management and layout improvements.

These tasks should be part of your project scope rather than unexpected expenses discovered just before launch.

7. Who builds your website

A freelancer, specialist studio and full-service agency may offer very different pricing structures.

A freelancer may handle design and development directly, making them suitable for focused projects.

A specialist studio may provide additional strategy, copywriting, SEO or project management.

A full-service agency may involve several specialists and a more extensive approval process.

The best choice depends on the complexity of your project and the expertise you actually need.

4. Freelancer vs. Web Design Agency: What Should You Pay?

Choosing the right development partner is one of the biggest financial decisions in a redesign.

Provider Illustrative project range Best for
DIY website builder $200–$1,000 in direct costs Simple websites and limited budgets
Freelance web designer $1,000–$7,500 Small businesses and focused redesigns
Specialist freelancer or studio $3,000–$15,000 Custom websites and technical projects
Full-service agency $10,000–$50,000+ Larger projects requiring multiple disciplines
Enterprise agency $50,000+ Complex, large-scale projects

When to hire a freelancer

A freelancer can be a cost-effective choice when your requirements are clear and one professional has the necessary expertise.

For example, a Webflow developer may be able to handle design implementation, responsive development, CMS setup and technical SEO for a relatively straightforward marketing website.

Before hiring, review their portfolio, relevant platform experience, communication process and approach to testing.

When to hire an agency

An agency may be preferable when your project requires coordinated expertise in branding, research, copywriting, UX design, development and digital marketing.

However, paying for a larger team doesn't automatically produce a better website.

Compare the actual deliverables, relevant experience and project management approach rather than choosing solely based on company size.

5. Webflow vs. Framer vs. WordPress: Which Is More Affordable to Redesign?

For a deeper platform comparison, read Webflow vs. Framer vs. WordPress: Which Is Best for Your Business in 2026?.

Your website platform influences both development costs and ongoing expenses.

Webflow redesign costs

Webflow is well suited to custom business websites, marketing websites and structured CMS projects.

A typical redesign may include custom layouts, reusable components, CMS collections, responsive development and marketing integrations.

For planning purposes, a straightforward small-business Webflow redesign might fall within a $2,000–$7,500 budget, while more complex custom projects can exceed $15,000.

These are illustrative development budgets, not official Webflow prices.

Webflow also charges for hosting and certain workspace or advanced features. Review its current pricing before selecting a plan.

Framer redesign costs

Framer is particularly attractive for startup websites, SaaS landing pages, portfolios and visually distinctive marketing websites.

Its design workflow can make some projects faster to develop, especially when the website has relatively simple content and functionality.

An illustrative budget for a focused Framer redesign is $1,500–$6,000. Larger projects with complex CMS structures, custom components or integrations can cost considerably more.

Framer's subscription and any paid add-ons are separate from development costs.

WordPress redesign costs

WordPress offers a wide range of development approaches.

A simple Elementor redesign may involve customizing an existing theme and improving responsive layouts.

A more complex project might require custom theme development, WooCommerce, advanced plugins, bespoke integrations or substantial content migration.

An illustrative budget for a straightforward WordPress redesign is $1,500–$7,500, while complex custom builds can exceed $20,000.

You'll also need to consider hosting, plugin licenses, updates, security and maintenance.

Which platform offers the best value?

For a design-focused startup website, Framer can offer excellent value.

For a custom marketing website with structured content, Webflow is often a strong option.

For highly customized websites, complex publishing requirements or ecommerce, WordPress may provide greater flexibility.

However, switching platforms purely to reduce the redesign quote can be a costly mistake. Evaluate migration expenses and long-term operating costs before making that decision.

7. Hidden Website Redesign Costs You Should Know About

A redesign quote may not include every expense associated with your website.

Before approving a proposal, check whether the following items are included.

Domain registration: Your domain is normally renewed annually, regardless of which platform you use.

Hosting and platform subscriptions: Webflow, Framer and WordPress have different hosting arrangements and subscription requirements.

Premium tools: Paid fonts, stock photography, premium plugins, analytics products and other third-party services can add recurring expenses.

Content migration: Transferring existing content may require substantial manual work, especially when the old and new CMS structures differ.

Copywriting and media: Professional copywriting, photography, video and custom illustrations are often separate services.

SEO migration: Redirects, technical audits and post-launch monitoring may not be included in a basic design package.

Maintenance: Ongoing support, updates, security and performance monitoring may be billed monthly or as needed.

Additional revisions: Changes outside the agreed scope may incur extra fees.

A useful proposal should distinguish one-time development costs from recurring expenses and clearly identify any exclusions.

8. How Long Does a Website Redesign Take?

A website redesign can take anywhere from a few days to several months.

Here are reasonable planning estimates for different types of projects.

Project Illustrative timeline
Minor visual refresh 3–10 working days
Five-page business redesign 2–4 weeks
Custom business website 4–8 weeks
SaaS marketing website 4–10 weeks
Complex migration or enterprise project 8–16+ weeks

Actual timelines depend on the complexity of the project, content readiness, stakeholder feedback and integrations.

A typical redesign involves five stages.

Stage 1: Discovery and planning

Define the website's goals, target audience, requirements, content structure and technical constraints.

Audit the existing website to identify what should be retained, improved or removed.

Stage 2: UX and visual design

Create wireframes, develop visual layouts and establish reusable components.

Review the design on desktop and mobile before beginning full development.

Stage 3: Development

Build the approved layouts, configure the CMS, add responsive behavior and implement integrations.

Stage 4: Testing and quality assurance

Test the website across devices and browsers.

Review forms, navigation, accessibility, performance, metadata and redirects.

Stage 5: Launch and monitoring

Publish the website, verify analytics and indexing, test redirects and monitor performance.

A successful launch should include a clear plan for resolving any issues discovered after publication.

9. Is a Website Redesign Worth the Investment?

A website redesign should solve a business problem, not simply make your website look different.

Consider a hypothetical service business receiving 2,000 website visits per month.

If its website converts 1% of visitors into inquiries, it generates approximately 20 inquiries each month.

If improved messaging, usability and conversion design eventually increase that rate to 2%, the website would generate 40 inquiries from the same amount of traffic.

That is an additional 20 inquiries per month.

However, this is an illustration, not a promised result. Actual performance depends on traffic quality, your offer, competition and the effectiveness of the redesign.

How to measure redesign ROI

Track relevant performance indicators before and after the project:

  • Qualified inquiries and conversion rate.
  • Organic search traffic and important keyword rankings.
  • Form completion and booking rates.
  • Mobile engagement and usability.
  • Website performance and Core Web Vitals.
  • Revenue attributable to website-generated leads.

For businesses that depend on online inquiries, improving conversion performance can be more valuable than simply increasing traffic.

10. When Should You Redesign Instead of Repairing Your Website?

Not every website needs a complete rebuild.

If your existing website has a strong structure and good content, targeted improvements may provide better value.

A refresh may be sufficient when your website needs updated typography, improved images, mobile spacing corrections or clearer calls to action.

A full redesign is more appropriate when your website no longer represents your business, its navigation is confusing, its content structure is ineffective or it consistently fails to support your marketing goals.

A platform migration may be justified when your existing CMS cannot support your content requirements, your marketing team struggles to make routine updates or the website requires substantial changes that are difficult to implement within the current system.

Before deciding, conduct a website audit covering design, performance, content, SEO, accessibility and conversion.

This will help you invest in improvements that address actual problems.

11. How to Reduce Website Redesign Costs Without Sacrificing Quality

A limited budget doesn't mean you have to settle for a poor website.

The key is prioritizing the work that matters most.

Start with your most important pages

You don't necessarily need to redesign every page at once.

Start with the homepage, primary service pages and the pages responsible for generating inquiries.

Less important sections can be improved in later phases.

Prepare your content before development

Collect your brand assets, images, testimonials, service descriptions and other essential content before the project begins.

This reduces delays and unnecessary revisions.

Use reusable components

Reusable layouts, navigation elements and CMS templates can reduce development time while improving consistency.

Avoid unnecessary integrations

Only implement tools that support a clear business requirement.

Additional integrations create development, testing and maintenance responsibilities.

Preserve what already works

If certain pages perform well in search or generate valuable inquiries, don't replace them without a reason.

Maintain successful content and functionality wherever possible.

Agree on a clear project scope

A detailed brief reduces misunderstandings and helps developers provide more accurate quotes.

Document the pages, functionality, content responsibilities, revision limits, testing requirements and launch deliverables.

12. What Should Be Included in a Professional Website Redesign Quote?

Before hiring a developer or agency, make sure the proposal clearly explains what you're purchasing.

A well-defined quote should identify the following:

  1. Project scope: The pages, templates and functionality being delivered.
  2. Design deliverables: Wireframes, visual designs, responsive layouts and revision rounds.
  3. Development requirements: Platform, CMS, integrations and custom functionality.
  4. Content responsibilities: Who provides, edits, imports and approves the content.
  5. SEO requirements: Metadata, redirects, sitemap and other relevant technical work.
  6. Testing: Browser compatibility, mobile responsiveness, forms and performance.
  7. Launch process: Publishing responsibilities, domain configuration and final checks.
  8. Timeline and payment terms: Milestones, approvals and payment schedule.
  9. Post-launch support: Bug fixes, training, maintenance and warranty terms.

Also confirm who owns the website, domain, content and third-party accounts after the project is complete.

The cheapest quote isn't necessarily the best value if essential services are excluded.

13. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to redesign a five-page website?

A straightforward five-page business website redesign might cost approximately $1,500–$5,000 when handled by a freelancer.

Custom design, professional copywriting, integrations and SEO migration can increase the price.

Is it cheaper to redesign a website or build a new one?

A simple refresh is usually cheaper than a new website.

However, a complete redesign may cost as much as or more than a new build because it can involve content migration, redirects and preserving existing functionality.

How much does a Webflow website redesign cost?

For planning purposes, a straightforward small-business Webflow redesign might fall within a $2,000–$7,500 development budget.

Complex custom projects may cost considerably more. Hosting and platform subscriptions are additional expenses.

How much does a Framer website redesign cost?

A focused Framer redesign might cost approximately $1,500–$6,000, depending on the design, page count, CMS and integrations.

Custom components and complex functionality can increase the cost.

How much does a WordPress website redesign cost?

A relatively straightforward WordPress redesign might cost $1,500–$7,500.

Projects involving custom development, ecommerce, extensive plugin customization or complex content migration can exceed $20,000.

Will redesigning my website affect SEO?

It can.

Changing URLs, removing valuable content or implementing redirects incorrectly can affect search visibility.

A properly planned redesign should include an SEO audit, redirect mapping and post-launch monitoring.

How often should a business redesign its website?

There is no universal schedule.

Redesign when your website no longer supports your business goals, rather than automatically replacing it every few years.

Regular audits and incremental improvements can extend the useful life of a well-built website.

Should I hire a freelancer or a web design agency?

Hire based on the expertise and resources your project requires.

A specialist freelancer may be an excellent choice for a focused Webflow, Framer or WordPress project.

A full-service agency may be preferable when you need coordinated branding, research, copywriting, design, development and marketing services.

Final Thoughts: How Much Should You Budget for Your Website Redesign?

A successful website redesign is an investment in your business's online presence, usability and ability to attract customers.

For many small businesses, an initial planning budget of $2,000–$15,000 is a useful starting point. A smaller refresh may cost substantially less, while a complex custom website or platform migration can require a much larger investment.

The most important thing is to define your goals before choosing a budget or hiring a developer.

Identify what isn't working, decide which improvements will have the greatest business impact and choose a development approach that supports your long-term requirements.

A well-planned redesign should leave you with more than an attractive website. It should provide a reliable platform for your marketing, content and business growth.
