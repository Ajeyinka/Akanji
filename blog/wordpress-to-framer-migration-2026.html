Migrating from WordPress to Framer can give your business a modern website, a streamlined visual editing experience and greater flexibility when designing interactive marketing pages.

However, moving an established website involves considerably more than copying its design into a new platform.

You need to transfer content, rebuild CMS collections, preserve important URLs, configure redirects, reconnect forms and integrations, and ensure that your website continues working after launch.

If your existing WordPress website generates organic traffic, a poorly planned migration could also affect your search visibility.

The safest way to migrate from WordPress to Framer is to audit your existing website, export and restructure your content, build and test the new website, map your URLs, and only then connect your production domain.

This guide walks you through the complete process, including common problems developers encounter when migrating WordPress blogs and CMS-driven websites.

1. Should You Migrate From WordPress to Framer?

Before starting, determine whether Framer is the right platform for your business.

WordPress and Framer serve different purposes.

WordPress is a highly extensible content management system with a large plugin ecosystem and considerable control over hosting and backend functionality.

Framer is a visual website development platform particularly suited to modern marketing websites, SaaS landing pages, portfolios and design-focused business websites.

When migrating to Framer makes sense

Consider Framer if your business needs:

  • A modern, custom-designed marketing website.
  • A streamlined workflow for publishing and updating pages.
  • Responsive layouts and sophisticated animations.
  • A manageable blog, portfolio or case study library.
  • Managed hosting without maintaining a traditional WordPress installation.
  • Faster visual experimentation for marketing campaigns.

For example, a SaaS startup with a WordPress website might want to move to Framer to simplify its marketing website and create visually engaging product pages.

A service business might make the same decision because it wants an easier way to update its website without maintaining numerous plugins.

When you should stay with WordPress

Framer isn't the best replacement for every WordPress website.

Reconsider the migration if your existing website relies heavily on WooCommerce, complex membership functionality, extensive custom post types, specialized plugins or custom server-side applications.

Some of these features can be recreated using third-party services or custom components, but the additional complexity may outweigh the benefits of switching platforms.

Recommendation: Audit your website's functionality before committing to Framer. A platform migration should solve existing problems, not introduce new ones.

3. Step 1: Audit Your Existing WordPress Website

Start by documenting everything your current website contains.

You need to know which pages exist, how they're organized, what functionality they provide and which pages attract valuable traffic.

Create a website inventory

List your existing URLs and classify them by page type.

For example:

Existing URL Page type Migration action
/ Homepage Rebuild
/about/ Static page Rebuild
/services/ Static page Rebuild
/blog/ Blog index Rebuild
/blog/example-post/ Blog post Import into CMS
/contact/ Form page Rebuild and reconnect
/old-service/ Outdated page Redirect or retire

You can create this inventory manually for a small website.

For larger websites, use a crawler such as Screaming Frog to identify URLs, page titles, descriptions, headings, images, canonical URLs and internal links.

Identify your most valuable pages

Not every page has the same business value.

Use Google Search Console and your analytics platform to identify pages that generate organic traffic, rank for valuable keywords, attract backlinks or produce inquiries.

These pages deserve additional attention during migration.

Audit existing functionality

Create a list of everything that depends on WordPress or its plugins.

Common examples include contact forms, newsletter subscriptions, booking systems, ecommerce, membership areas, custom search, analytics and CRM integrations.

For each feature, determine whether Framer has a native equivalent or whether you'll need a third-party service or custom development.

Record your SEO baseline

Before making changes, record:

  • Organic traffic and important search queries.
  • Indexed pages.
  • Existing URL structure.
  • Page titles and meta descriptions.
  • Canonical URLs.
  • Structured data.
  • Important backlinks.
  • Core Web Vitals and page performance.

This gives you a baseline against which to evaluate the migration.

4. Step 2: Back Up Your WordPress Website

Never begin a migration without a reliable backup.

Your backup should include the WordPress database, uploaded media, themes, custom code and relevant configuration files.

You can create backups through your hosting provider, a trusted backup plugin or your server's backup facilities.

Export your WordPress content

Log in to your WordPress dashboard and navigate to:

Tools → Export → All Content → Download Export File

WordPress generates an XML file in its WordPress eXtended RSS format, commonly called WXR.

The export can contain posts, pages, custom post types, comments, custom fields, categories, tags and other supported content.

However, a WordPress XML export is not a complete website backup. It does not independently preserve your entire hosting environment, theme functionality or all plugin-specific data.

Keep a separate full backup.

Export additional information

Depending on your website, you may also need separate exports of SEO metadata, form submissions, ecommerce records, plugin settings and custom database tables.

Download your media files or ensure that you have a reliable way to access them during migration.

Don't cancel your WordPress hosting immediately after exporting. Keep the original website available until the new site has been launched and verified.

5. Step 3: Export WordPress Posts to CSV

WordPress's standard export produces XML, while spreadsheet-based CMS migration workflows generally require CSV or another structured format.

You'll therefore need to transform your exported content.

There are several ways to approach this.

Option A: Use a WordPress export plugin

A suitable export plugin can simplify the process by allowing you to select fields and export posts directly to CSV.

For example, you may need:

  • Post title.
  • URL slug.
  • Publication date.
  • Author.
  • Category.
  • Excerpt.
  • Main content.
  • Featured image.
  • SEO title and description.

Review the plugin's supported fields before exporting.

Option B: Use the WordPress REST API

For developers, the WordPress REST API provides a programmable way to retrieve content.

A typical posts endpoint looks like this:

https://example.com/wp-json/wp/v2/posts

You can request additional pages of results and retrieve relevant fields programmatically.

For example:

https://example.com/wp-json/wp/v2/posts?per_page=100&page=1

If your website contains more than 100 posts, paginate through the results until you've retrieved everything.

Be aware that some custom fields and plugin-generated metadata may not be exposed through the public API without additional configuration.

Option C: Convert the WordPress XML export

You can also parse the XML file and convert the required fields into CSV.

This approach can be useful when you have an existing export or limited access to the live WordPress installation.

However, rich text, embedded media and plugin-specific fields may require additional processing.

Example CSV structure

A simple blog export might look like this:

Title Slug Category Featured Image
Choosing a Website Platform choosing-a-platform Web Design image-01.jpg
Improving Website Speed improve-website-speed Performance image-02.jpg
Website SEO Checklist website-seo-checklist SEO image-03.jpg

Your final export should also include the full article content, publication dates, descriptions and any additional fields required by your Framer CMS.

Before importing, open the CSV and check for broken characters, missing values, duplicate slugs and incorrectly formatted dates.

6. Step 4: Plan Your Framer CMS Structure

One of the most important parts of a successful migration is mapping your WordPress content to Framer's CMS.

Don't import everything into a single collection without considering how the content will be used.

For a typical business website, you might create separate collections for blog posts, case studies, team members and products.

Example: Blog collection

WordPress field Suggested Framer field
Post title Title
Post slug Slug
Post content Rich Text
Excerpt Excerpt
Featured image Image
Publication date Date
Category Option or reference
Author Text or reference
SEO title SEO Title
Meta description SEO Description

The exact field types depend on your project's requirements and the Framer CMS features available on your plan.

Handle categories and authors carefully

WordPress supports taxonomies and complex content relationships.

When moving to Framer, decide whether each category should be represented as a simple option or a separate collection.

A separate collection may be appropriate when categories need their own images, descriptions or landing pages.

Similarly, a separate Authors collection may be useful if your website has multiple contributors with individual biography pages.

What about custom post types?

If your WordPress website uses custom post types, such as Projects, Testimonials or Products, review each one separately.

For example, a Projects collection might contain a project title, industry, featured image, gallery, description, services and client testimonial.

Some custom fields may need to be restructured rather than imported directly.

The goal is to create a content model that works naturally in Framer, not to reproduce every detail of your WordPress database.

7. Step 5: Build Your New Website in Framer

Once your CMS architecture is planned, start building your new website.

Create a Framer project and decide whether to use a template or develop a custom design.

If your existing website already has a strong visual identity, you may want to preserve its branding while improving its layout, typography and responsiveness.

Build reusable components

Start with components that appear throughout your website, including navigation, footer, buttons, testimonials, cards and calls to action.

Reusable components improve consistency and make future changes easier.

Establish responsive layouts

Test your designs at desktop, tablet and mobile widths.

Pay particular attention to navigation, text wrapping, image proportions, content spacing and interactive elements.

Don't assume that a desktop design will automatically provide a good mobile experience.

Create CMS templates

Build templates for your blog posts, case studies and other dynamic content.

For a blog, you'll typically need an index page displaying articles and a detail template displaying the full content of each post.

Connect the appropriate CMS fields to the relevant text, image and metadata elements.

Preserve important content

A migration can also be an opportunity to improve your website.

However, avoid removing valuable content simply because it doesn't fit the new design.

If a page attracts organic traffic or generates qualified inquiries, preserve its useful information and search intent.

8. Step 6: Import Your Content Into Framer CMS

After creating your collections and templates, you can begin importing content.

For a small website, manually creating CMS items may be manageable.

For a larger website, a structured import workflow is more efficient.

Framer's CMS tools and supported import workflows can help transfer spreadsheet-based content into collections. Check the available import capabilities in your current Framer environment before choosing a method.

Prepare your import file

Ensure that each column corresponds to the intended Framer field.

Remove unnecessary WordPress-specific markup and check that your rich text is compatible with your chosen import workflow.

Pay particular attention to headings, lists, links, embedded media and code blocks.

Import and verify your content

Begin with a small sample rather than importing your entire website immediately.

For example, import three posts with different content structures:

  1. A simple article containing text and headings.
  2. An article containing images and lists.
  3. A more complex article containing embedded media or custom formatting.

Check how each appears in your Framer template.

Once the sample content looks correct, proceed with the remaining posts.

Troubleshooting common import problems

Missing images: Check whether the import workflow expects hosted image URLs, uploaded assets or another supported format.

Incorrect dates: Standardize date formats before importing.

Broken formatting: Review the source HTML and remove unsupported WordPress shortcodes or plugin-specific markup.

Missing categories: Verify option values or create the required reference items before importing.

Duplicate slugs: Ensure every item has a unique URL slug.

Broken references: Import dependent collections first, then connect their relationships.

Framer's AI-assisted CMS import workflows can also read CSV files and map columns, but imported dates, references, options and images still require manual verification.

9. Step 7: Migrate Images and Media

Images are frequently overlooked during website migrations.

Your WordPress website may contain featured images, inline article images, galleries, downloadable PDFs and other media.

Start by creating an inventory of the assets you need to preserve.

Featured images

Transfer the original images where possible and use suitable formats and resolutions for the new website.

Avoid repeatedly downloading and recompressing already optimized images.

Inline article images

Check that every image embedded in an article still loads correctly.

If your imported content contains image URLs pointing to the old WordPress domain, decide whether to retain those URLs temporarily or replace them with assets hosted through your new website.

Keeping images on an old WordPress server can create broken content if that hosting is later cancelled.

Galleries and multi-image fields

Complex WordPress galleries may not map directly to your chosen Framer CMS structure.

You may need to transform the source data, create a supported gallery representation or develop a custom component.

Test the entire gallery workflow before migrating large numbers of items.

PDFs and downloadable resources

Re-upload downloadable assets to a reliable location and update the links in your content.

Check whether important document URLs need redirects.

10. Step 8: Preserve Your SEO During Migration

A website migration can affect search visibility, particularly when page URLs or content change.

The safest approach is to preserve existing URLs wherever practical and implement permanent redirects when URLs must change.

Create a URL mapping spreadsheet

Map every important old URL to its intended destination.

For example:

Old WordPress URL New Framer URL Action
/about-us/ /about/ 301 redirect
/our-services/ /services/ 301 redirect
/blog/website-tips/ /blog/website-tips/ Preserve URL
/old-service/ /services/ Redirect if relevant

Avoid redirecting unrelated pages to the homepage.

Where a page has been replaced, redirect it to the most relevant equivalent. Where no equivalent exists, decide whether to preserve the content, consolidate it into another page or return an appropriate removal status.

Configure Framer redirects

In Framer, navigate to:

Site Settings → Hosting → Redirects

Enter the old path and its new destination, then publish or update your website.

Framer also supports wildcard rules for suitable URL patterns.

For example, if your old website used /articles/ and the new website uses /blog/, a carefully configured wildcard rule may simplify the migration.

Test wildcard rules against actual URLs before applying them broadly.

Transfer metadata

Preserve or deliberately improve the SEO title, meta description, canonical URL and relevant structured data for each important page.

Configure dynamic metadata for CMS templates where appropriate.

Check your sitemap and indexing settings

Framer automatically generates a sitemap and robots.txt for published websites.

After launch, inspect these files and confirm that important pages are discoverable.

Also check that your pages aren't accidentally configured to prevent indexing.

Review canonical URLs

Framer generally generates canonical tags using the connected domain.

If you're using an unusual hosting or reverse-proxy arrangement, verify that canonical URLs point to the correct public domain.

Don't promise zero ranking changes

Even a carefully executed migration may produce temporary fluctuations in organic traffic or indexing.

Your goal is to minimize avoidable problems and monitor the transition, not guarantee unchanged rankings.

11. Step 9: Reconnect Forms, Analytics and Integrations

Your new website needs to do more than display content.

Before launch, make sure every important business workflow works correctly.

Contact forms

Recreate your contact forms in Framer and connect them to the appropriate destination.

Depending on your requirements, this might be an email notification, CRM, Google Sheets or automation platform.

Submit several test entries and verify that the data reaches the intended destination.

CRM and email marketing

If your WordPress website previously connected to HubSpot, Mailchimp or another marketing platform, rebuild and test those integrations.

Check field mappings, consent requirements, notifications and automation triggers.

Booking systems

Reconnect Calendly or any other scheduling service.

Test the booking process on desktop and mobile.

Analytics and conversion tracking

Install or configure your preferred analytics platform and verify that page views and important conversion events are being recorded.

If you use Google Analytics or Google Tag Manager, ensure that the migration doesn't accidentally introduce duplicate tracking.

Also preserve any relevant cookie consent or privacy requirements.

12. Step 10: Test Your Framer Website Before Launch

Testing is one of the most important stages of the migration.

Don't connect your production domain until the new website has passed a thorough review.

Functional testing

Test navigation, internal links, forms, buttons, downloads, CMS pages and interactive components.

Verify that every important user journey works.

Responsive testing

Review your website on desktop, tablet and mobile.

Check different screen widths rather than testing only one device.

Content testing

Compare imported content against the original WordPress website.

Check titles, publication dates, featured images, headings, formatting, internal links and downloadable assets.

SEO testing

Verify page titles, meta descriptions, canonical tags, redirects, sitemap configuration and indexing settings.

Use a website crawler to identify broken links and missing metadata.

Performance testing

Run important pages through Google PageSpeed Insights.

Review image sizes, unnecessary scripts, layout stability and mobile performance.

Prioritize actual user experience rather than chasing a perfect Lighthouse score.

13. Step 11: Connect Your Domain and Launch

Once your website is ready, connect your custom domain through Framer.

Navigate to your project's domain settings and select the option to connect a domain you own.

Framer will provide the DNS records required for your configuration.

Update the records through your domain registrar or DNS provider, following Framer's current instructions.

DNS changes can take time to propagate, so plan your launch accordingly.

Important: Protect your email configuration

If your domain also handles business email, avoid changing unrelated MX, SPF, DKIM or DMARC records.

A website migration should not interrupt your email service.

Launch checklist

Before announcing your new website, confirm that:

  • The custom domain loads correctly over HTTPS.
  • Important pages are accessible.
  • Old URLs redirect to the correct destinations.
  • Contact forms and integrations work.
  • Analytics and conversion tracking are active.
  • Canonical URLs point to the intended domain.
  • The sitemap is accessible.
  • The website works properly on mobile.

Keep your original WordPress website and backups available during the initial post-launch period.

14. Step 12: Monitor Your Website After Migration

Publishing your new website isn't the end of the migration.

Monitor it closely during the following weeks.

First 24–48 hours

Check your domain, redirects, forms, analytics, important pages and any unexpected errors.

Resolve critical problems immediately.

First week

Review Google Search Console for indexing issues, crawl errors and changes in search visibility.

Inspect your most important landing pages and verify that internal links point to the new URLs.

First month

Compare organic traffic, conversions, indexed pages and Core Web Vitals against your pre-migration baseline.

Investigate unexpected changes rather than assuming every fluctuation is caused by the platform migration.

Following months

Continue monitoring your search performance and update redirects, content or technical settings where necessary.

Don't remove important redirects simply because the new website has launched successfully.

15. Common WordPress to Framer Migration Mistakes

Several avoidable mistakes can turn a straightforward migration into a costly project.

Migrating without a complete backup

Always preserve a recoverable copy of your original website before making changes.

Ignoring the existing URL structure

Changing URLs without redirects can break backlinks, internal links and existing search results.

Importing content without testing

Import a representative sample before transferring hundreds of CMS items.

Forgetting plugin-dependent functionality

A WordPress export won't automatically reproduce features created by plugins.

Audit those features and plan suitable replacements.

Leaving images on the old hosting server

Ensure that important media remains accessible after the old WordPress hosting is discontinued.

Launching without testing forms

A visually perfect website can still lose leads if its forms and integrations aren't working.

Cancelling WordPress hosting too early

Keep your original website available until you've confirmed that the migration is complete and that no essential data or functionality has been overlooked.

16. How Much Does It Cost to Migrate WordPress to Framer?

Migration costs vary considerably depending on the website's size, design and functionality.

For budgeting purposes, a small business migration may cost approximately $1,500–$5,000, while a larger custom migration with complex CMS content and integrations may require $5,000–$15,000 or more.

These are illustrative planning ranges rather than fixed market rates.

The main cost factors include the number of unique pages, volume of content, design requirements, custom fields, integrations and SEO migration complexity.

A simple five-page website will generally be less expensive to migrate than a content-heavy website with hundreds of posts and extensive custom functionality.

Before requesting a quote, prepare an inventory of your pages, content and integrations.

17. Frequently Asked Questions

Can I migrate WordPress to Framer automatically?

Some content can be transferred using exports, CSV transformations, import tools and automation.

However, a complete migration generally requires manual work to rebuild layouts, configure the CMS, reconnect functionality and validate the finished website.

Can I import WordPress blog posts into Framer?

Yes. You can export WordPress content, transform it into a compatible format and import it using an appropriate Framer CMS workflow.

Check rich text, images, dates, categories and references after importing.

Will I lose my Google rankings if I move to Framer?

Not necessarily.

Preserving valuable content, maintaining existing URLs where possible and implementing correct redirects can reduce migration-related SEO risks.

However, no migration process can guarantee that rankings will remain unchanged.

Can I keep my existing domain?

Yes. You can connect your existing domain to Framer by updating the appropriate DNS records.

You don't need to purchase a new domain simply because you're changing website platforms.

Can Framer replace Elementor?

Framer can replace Elementor for many marketing websites, portfolios and business websites.

However, it doesn't reproduce every WordPress or Elementor feature, so audit your existing functionality before migrating.

How long does a WordPress to Framer migration take?

A small website may take one to three weeks, while a larger website with custom design, extensive CMS content and complex integrations can take several weeks or longer.

The timeline depends on project scope, content readiness and testing requirements.

Is Framer better than WordPress for SEO?

Both can support strong technical SEO.

Framer provides managed hosting and built-in SEO controls, while WordPress offers extensive customization through plugins and custom development.

The quality of your content and implementation matters more than the platform alone.

Final Thoughts: Is Moving From WordPress to Framer Worth It?

Migrating from WordPress to Framer can be a valuable investment when your business needs a modern, design-focused website and a streamlined content management experience.

However, the migration should be approached as a complete website project rather than a simple platform switch.

Audit your existing website, preserve your content, plan your CMS, implement redirects and thoroughly test every important feature before launch.

If you follow a structured migration process, you can take advantage of Framer's visual development capabilities while reducing unnecessary disruption to your existing website.
